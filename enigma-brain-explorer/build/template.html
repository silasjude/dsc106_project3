About the data

How to read this

Each condition was studied by an ENIGMA working group that pooled MRI scans from dozens of research sites. For every brain area, they compared the average size of that area in people with the condition with healthy people of similar age and sex.

Colors

  • Blue means thinner or smaller on average in people with the condition. Orange means thicker or larger.
  • Brighter means a bigger difference. Dark grey means no reliable difference.
  • In the overview, brighter yellow means more conditions differ in that area.

Size of a difference

Differences are Cohen's d: the gap between group averages in units of normal person-to-person variation. About 0.2 is small, 0.5 moderate and 0.8 large. The percentages translate d into plain terms, assuming roughly bell-shaped distributions: a d of −0.5 means the typical person with the condition is thinner than about 69% of healthy people.

Reliable

A difference is reliable when it passes the study's false-discovery-rate correction (q < .05). Where only uncorrected p-values were released (Parkinson's disease; deep structures in ADHD and depression), p < .05 is used. An area counts toward the overview when its difference is reliable and at least small (|d| ≥ 0.2).

Keep in mind

  • These are group averages. Brains vary widely and no one can be diagnosed from them.
  • Size differences do not show cause. Medication, illness duration and other factors can contribute.
  • Some older deep-structure results report the average of left and right, so both sides show the same value.
  • Studies differ in size, age range and statistical models, so compare patterns across conditions, not exact numbers.

Sources

Values from the ENIGMA Toolbox (Larivière et al., 2021, Nature Methods), Desikan–Killiany atlas: 68 cortical areas and 16 deep structures.
