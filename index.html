Why would clearing forests heat the ground?

A rainforest is crucial for keeping the ground cooler. Trees take water from the soil and release it through their leaves. When that water evaporates, it carries heat away from the ground, cooling the surface. Because pasture and cropland move much less water compared to forests, more of the sun's energy goes into heating the surface.

Since 2000, NASA's MODIS instrument on the Terra satellite has tracked how green the land is using a Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) and how hot the land surface is at about 10:30 in the morning, local time. Each 5 km square contains both numbers for every dry season from 2001 to 2022.

How the numbers were made

  1. Source. NASA MODIS on the Terra satellite. We used monthly NDVI (with 1 km detail) and monthly daytime land surface temperature (with 5 km detail) for July to September of every year. We downloaded the data through NASA GIBS and decoded it from color back to values using each layer's published colormap.
  2. Grid. The grid is formed by an area from 75°W to 45°W and 18°S to 2°N, split into 0.05° squares (roughly 5.5 km). A square's NDVI is the mean of the ~25 one-km pixels inside it. This averages the finer NDVI data to make the tooltip less sensitive.
  3. Dry season. Each square gets one value per year (the median of the July, August and September composites). The dry season has the fewest clouds, and is when pasture and forest differ the most.
  4. Change. Measured by the selected year's value minus that square's own 2001–2003 average. The map shows a 3-year average ending in the selected year, so a drought year (2005, 2010, 2015–16) does not skew the general picture.
  5. Cleared. A square counts as cleared when its greenness fell more than 0.20 below baseline.
  6. Why end at 2022? Terra's orbit has been shifting since 2022, so it passes earlier in the morning when the ground is cooler. Because of this, our control box (untouched forest) appears to be 1–2 °C cooler in 2025–26.

Limits. Squares 5km large can blur smaller clearings. A drop in NDVI can also come from fire or drought (the 3-year rule and the stable comparison minimize this but the issue is not eliminated). Also, LST is a skin temperature at one time of day, not the air temperature.
