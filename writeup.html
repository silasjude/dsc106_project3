Motivation

[2–4 sentences, your words: why we picked this question and what a reader should take away.]

  • Question: does losing forest cover make the land surface measurably hotter, and by how much?
  • Mechanism to mention: forests cool the surface through evapotranspiration; pasture moves less water, so more sunlight becomes heat.
  • Headline result (2020–22 vs 2001–03, dry season): cleared squares warmed about 3.8 °C more than stable forest nearby in Rondônia and about 4.0 °C more along BR-163 in Pará. Same as our checkpoint notebook (3.83 and 4.01).
  • Link back to the checkpoint: we started from global NDVI/LST maps, narrowed to the Amazon, then to the cleared-vs-stable comparison.

Interaction techniques

[For each technique: what it lets the reader discover that a static chart couldn't.]

  • Time slider + play (dynamic query over 2001–2022): shows clearing spreading out from roads, which a single before/after map hides.
  • Brushing and linking: box on the map → scatter, time series and readout update for that area. Box on the scatter → only those squares stay lit on the map (e.g. box the "cleared" tail and you see the fishbone road pattern).
  • Details on demand: hover tooltip with a square's 22-year greenness and temperature history; you can see the year it was cleared and the temperature jump right after.
  • Presets (Rondônia, Pará, intact control): one click to the comparisons from our analysis; the control shows the background trend.
  • Guided tour (6 steps): narrative layer that sets the state for each finding; the reader can leave at any point and explore.
  • Satellite swipe in the header: real MODIS true-color images 2001 vs 2021, as a hook before the data.

Alternatives we considered

[Pick the ones you actually discussed; add your own.]

  • Two side-by-side static maps (NDVI change, LST change): clear, but no way to quantify the link or look at a sub-region.
  • A plain point scatter: unreadable at this size; a hexbin needs the d3-hexbin plugin (not core D3), so we used square bins.
  • One dual-axis chart of NDVI and LST over time: rejected, since dual axes suggest correlations through arbitrary scaling.
  • Single-year frames: too noisy (2005, 2010, 2015–16 droughts) → 3-year trailing average.
  • Extending to 2026: rejected after finding the Terra orbit drift (control forest "cools" 1–2 °C in 2025–26).
  • CMIP6 model projections: we wanted observed change, so we used MODIS.

Data transformations

[Short summary; the full list is on the main page under "How the numbers were made".]

  • GIBS PNGs decoded back to values with each layer's colormap; NDVI requested at 0.01° and averaged into 0.05° blocks; LST at native ~0.05°.
  • Dry season = Jul–Sep; per-year median of the 3 monthly composites; baseline = 2001–03 mean; change = year − baseline; displayed as a 3-year trailing mean.
  • Cleared: NDVI change < −0.20; stable: |change| < 0.05; "year cleared" needs the drop to persist 3 years (notebook rule).
  • Clearing effect = mean ΔLST(cleared) − mean ΔLST(stable) in the same area, which cancels regional warming and drought.
  • Not shown: water and cells with too few valid 1 km pixels (gray).
